02 / My story

From curiosity to creation.

A personal learning journey shaped by experimentation, problem-solving and the motivation to make ideas real.

CHAPTER 01 — THE BEGINNING

Learning by doing

My interest in technology grew as I explored how websites and applications are designed and built. Instead of only looking at the finished interface, I became curious about the code and logic behind it.

Every new experiment gives me a chance to learn something different, whether it is structuring a page, connecting a service or improving a user interface.

CHAPTER 02 — THE PROCESS

Building through challenges

Working on personal projects has introduced me to the practical side of development: debugging, understanding errors, testing changes and learning how different technologies work together.

Progress does not always happen in a straight line. I treat mistakes as part of the process and keep refining my approach as my knowledge grows.

CHAPTER 03 — THE MOTIVATION

An idea worth exploring

My English teacher once suggested that I consider joining Coolest Projects. Although I did not end up participating, the suggestion became one of the experiences I associate with taking my interest in technology more seriously.

It remains a reminder that an idea can be a starting point for learning, even when the original opportunity does not become a completed event.

CHAPTER 04 — WHAT COMES NEXT

Keep moving forward

I want to continue developing my skills, understanding software architecture and building projects that are more reliable, useful and thoughtfully designed.

My long-term direction is to grow through consistent practice, meaningful projects and a willingness to keep learning.

Coolest Projects note: This portfolio records the teacher's suggestion only. It does not claim that I registered, participated, presented a project or received an award.

05 / Working principles

How I approach development.

The principles I want to keep improving as I build and learn.

01. Understand the problem

Start with the purpose of a feature before adding complexity to the interface.

02. Build in steps

Break a large idea into smaller tasks that are easier to implement, test and understand.

03. Test honestly

Distinguish between a working feature, a prototype and something that still needs development.

04. Keep learning

Use errors and feedback as opportunities to understand the technology more deeply.
